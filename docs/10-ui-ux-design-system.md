# 10 — UI/UX & Design System

## 1. Design language

Clean, whitespace-heavy, card-based; the AI is the hero feature but nothing depends on it. Reference feel: *Linear's calm + Stripe Dashboard's data density, tuned for school administrators — friendly, not fintech-cold.*

### Tokens (source of truth)

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cyan-Teal | `#00a5cb` | Primary actions, primary chart series. **Meadow, 2026-09-03** (was `#008a9d`, and `#028090` before that) |
| Ink | `#0d2a31` | Headings, sidebar/nav, dark bands (lifted with the palette so the chrome is not a generation behind the charts) |
| Seafoam | `#4a913b` | Secondary accents (meadow's deep ramp step) |
| Meadow | `#72ba63` | Success, active/ON states, "collected" |
| Ochre | `#a17a00` | Warnings, fees-outstanding, third chart series. **The darkest colour in the set, and that is load-bearing** — green and this collapse to one hue under protanopia and deuteranopia, so only their lightness gap separates them |
| Rose | `#cd617e` | Errors, defaulter counts, FALSE branches |
| Violet | `#a886e5` | The fifth chart series. No fixed meaning |
| Slate `#334155` / Muted `#64748B` | Body text / captions |
| Canvas `#F1F5F9` / Card `#FFFFFF` | Page background / cards |

Type: **Inter**, one scale — Page title 28 semibold · Card heading 18 semibold · Body/table 14 · Caption/kicker 11 (+2 tracking); tabular figures for all numbers. Components: 8 px radius cards with soft shadows; primary (filled teal) / secondary (teal outline) / ghost buttons; chips; KPI cards (big number + delta pill); switches.

**Surfaces amended 2026-09-03**, alongside the chart-paint amendment in principle 2 below and for the same reason — the charts got quieter, and chrome that was merely *present* before became the loudest thing on the page once it no longer had gloss to compete with. Card radius **14 px** (from 12: at the 340–720 px a panel occupies, 12 reads as a square with its corners knocked off, and chips keep the 7 px `--radius-sm`, so "small control" and "surface" are now visibly different rather than two pixels apart). Card shadows roughly **halved** — a dashboard is cards on a tinted canvas and the canvas already separates them; at the old weights eleven panels read as a stack of floating slabs. `--shadow-float` is untouched: a popover really is floating. Panel padding 20/22 and grid gap **20 px** (from 16), because a quiet card takes its definition from its whitespace rather than its edges. A KPI figure is **29 px** (from 26) over an 11 px tracked kicker on a 3 px rhythm — the figure is the reason the tile exists and was sitting at barely twice its own label. A table's header is a **label strip, not a filled band**: sticky and card-coloured with a single rule under smaller, tracked, muted type, where it used to carry a grey fill that was the loudest horizontal element on a card whose subject is the rows below it; rows go to 12 px of vertical padding with a lighter divider, since a column of figures is scanned down and what makes that easy is air between lines, not rules.

**Surfaces amended again 2026-09-03 (third pass, "Meadow Lit").** Two of the numbers above had already been corrected in code without the doc following, and this records where they landed. Card radius is **12 px** — the second pass took 14 → 8 arguing that a rounder panel detaches from its neighbours and floats, which is right about the effect and wrong about the cause: what makes eleven panels float is the *shadow*, and that is where the argument now applies. Panel padding is **12/14** and the grid gap **12 px** (not 20): a reporting surface is read by comparing panels against each other, and comparison wants them close — the 1px borders do the separating. A KPI figure is **24 px**, because presence comes from contrast with its 11 px kicker rather than absolute size, and 29 px was setting the height of the strip that pushes every chart below the fold.

What is new in this pass is **tint**. Card shadows stay hairline-weight but gain a wide, very soft second layer, so a white card on a tinted canvas has form without lifting off it. The canvas itself carries three barely-there hue pools (`--grad-canvas`, all under 10% alpha) rather than being flat grey; a card falls about 1.5% toward the canvas from top to bottom; the sidebar gains a long vertical fall through the ink with the brand teal pooled behind the mark; and the topbar is **frosted** (82% white + blur) rather than opaque, because it is sticky and content scrolls under it. Every wash in the product is drawn at **135°** from one token set (`--grad-teal/mint/amber/rose/violet`, plus `--grad-ai`), built from the same `RAMP` steps `packages/chart-spec` paints marks with — one light source, one construction, so a KPI tile's accent and the bar summarising the same measure cannot drift apart. `--grad-ai` (teal → violet) is the one place two hues are deliberately crossed, and it is reserved for the AI surface, which reports no measure and therefore can carry no measure's colour.

### Principles

1. **Calm surfaces** — data is the color, chrome is quiet.
2. **One chart language** — every report, custom report, AI artifact and PDF draws through one renderer, so they cannot drift (a direct consequence of spec-driven rendering, doc 05). **Amended 2026-09-01.** Categorical series are **five** steps (`#008a9d`, `#3db575`, `#c78100`, `#c74859`, `#4967c6`), each with a three-step ramp (deep / base / lit) used to shade marks. The prior four-step set was capped at four because a 2026-08-22 audit found two teal-family steps indistinguishable at mark size, and §1.2's "teal-family series" rule ruled out reaching outside the family to fix the spacing. That rule is what is amended: **hue variety is now allowed beyond the teal family**, because leaving the family is exactly what buys the separation the old audit could not find inside it — indigo is nowhere near teal, where a second teal step never could be. The slot ORDER remains semantic and is not permutable (demand, collected, pending, defaulters), so hues are pushed apart within their families and lightness carries the separation that hue cannot: under protanopia and deuteranopia green and amber collapse to one hue, and only their lightness gap survives. Measured on the shipped set (`scripts/validate_palette.js`): worst adjacent pair ΔE 9.1 deutan / 9.8 tritan, worst normal-vision pair ΔE 16.2, against floors of 8 and 15; all five inside the lightness band and above the chroma floor, which the old set failed on two slots and one slot respectively. One accepted deviation, stated rather than hidden: `#3db575` sits at 2.54:1 against the card, under the 3:1 relief line — the remedy is visible labels or a table view, and every multi-series chart here carries a mandatory legend, axis values and a tooltip. The old palette failed that check on **two** slots. A category past the fifth is still never a generated hue; it folds into a recessive "Other" fill (`#64748b` at reduced opacity), alternating with `#cbd5e1` inside a stack.

    **Amended 2026-09-03 (second) — the palette itself is now "Meadow", and marks are FLAT.** Chosen from four directions put side by side on real Fee Collection figures. The brief was softer, less dark colours; the finding that shaped the answer is that **a five-slot categorical palette cannot simply be washed out to get there.** Four hand-picked pastel sets were measured and all four failed: draining chroma dropped them below the 0.10 floor at which a colour starts reading grey, and it collapsed teal↔green to ΔE 10 against a normal-vision floor of 15 — a pair *full-colour* readers cannot separate. So softness is bought with **lightness and with how the mark is drawn**, never by draining hue. Measured on the shipped set (`scripts/validate_palette.js`, surface `#ffffff`): worst adjacent CVD pair ΔE 10.4 deutan, worst normal-vision pair ΔE 16.3, all five inside the lightness band and above the chroma floor — every check passes. One accepted deviation, unchanged in kind from before: `#72ba63` sits at 2.35:1 against the card, under the 3:1 relief line, and the remedy is the relief this product already ships — a mandatory legend on every multi-series chart, axis values, and a table view. The old palette failed that check on two slots.

    Three treatment rules come with it, and they replace the shading rules amended earlier the same day:

    - **A mark is one colour — the one in the legend.** *(Superseded by the third amendment below, which restores the wash with the legend swatch washed to match; the rule's REASON — a mark must be matchable against its key — is what the replacement satisfies.)* Bars carry no gradient at all now. They have been through three treatments and the reasoning ran out in a useful direction: a five-stop cylinder read as plumbing, and a two-stop sheen still meant a reader matching a bar to its legend dot was matching a colour to the *midpoint of a wash*. Donut slices are flat for the same reason. There is no `<defs>` on a bar chart any more.
    - **The tonal interest moves behind the mark, into a rail.** Every bar sits in its own hue at **10%**, spanning the full scale (`RAIL_STRENGTH`). This is not the grey track removed on 2026-09-01: fourteen grey rails behind fourteen teal bars read as a striped table with the data faint inside it, the scale louder than the measurement. A rail *in the hue* reads instead as the unfilled part of the same measure, so a short bar is a small share of something rather than a stub floating in space. Never on a **stacked** bar, whose segments are parts of one bar — a full-scale rail behind each would draw four scales for one measurement.
    - **The rails replace the grid on bar charts.** A rail per mark and a rule per tick are two scales drawn over each other. Line charts keep their dashed grid, because nothing else there says where the scale runs.

    The `toneColour` map that paints a KPI figure now reads its four values **out of `SERIES` by slot**. It had drifted two palette generations behind and was painting a tile and the chart summarising the same measure in visibly different colours; reading it from the source means the next palette change cannot leave it behind again.

    **Amended 2026-09-03 — how a mark is PAINTED, which is a separate question from which hue it takes.** The palette above is unchanged; every hue, its slot and its semantics stand. What changed is the treatment, after a review against a reference dashboard whose charts read markedly cleaner than ours at the same data density. Four rules now bind, all in `packages/chart-spec/src/react/widgets.tsx`:

    - **A mark is its hue, not a rendering of a solid.** A bar's fill is a **two-stop** gradient across its thickness — the ramp's new half-step `soft` at the lit edge, `base` at the far edge — and a donut slice takes the same two stops. It was a five-stop cylinder (deep → base → lit → base → deep) plus a hue-coloured drop shadow, and that read exactly as designed: as tubes. The whole of a bar's meaning is where its end lands, and shading that models a *surface* spends the reader's attention on the middle of the mark; three tubes side by side in a grouped chart also fought each other, and fourteen of them read as plumbing. The two-stop version keeps the one thing the shading bought — a lit edge, so a mark sits on the card rather than being a hole cut in it — and drops the rest. It also fixes a real defect: a five-stop wash ending on `deep` meant a reader matching a bar against its legend dot was matching two different colours. The `deep` and `lit` steps remain, for pressed states, KPI tile edges and the donut. The ramp gains **`soft`** (`#2f9eb0`, `#5fc98e`, `#d99833`, `#d96471`, `#607ed6`) because painting a 22px bar from `lit` made it visibly two-tone.
    - **A single-series bar chart shades its bars along the series' own ramp by magnitude**, `soft` at the bottom of the range to `deep` at the top, measured from zero exactly as the bar length is. This is *not* the "paint the tallest bar darker" device removed on 2026-09-01: that singled out one mark on a single-measure chart and therefore read as a second series. A continuous ramp picks out nothing, groups nothing, and is a function of the same number the length already encodes — redundant with it, which is what makes it safe. It exists because fourteen classes in one flat teal is a picket fence with no focus, and single-series bar charts are what this product has most of.
    - **Chrome is subtracted from every chart.** Grid lines are dashed (`3 4`) and lightened to `#e3e8ef` — a grid is a ruler, and a solid rule inside a card that already has a border reads as a second box. Axis lines and tick stubs are gone entirely: the card is the frame and the grid is the scale, so a third boundary says nothing new. The per-bar background "track" is gone — on three schools it framed the marks, on fourteen classes it drew fourteen full-width grey rails behind the data and the panel read as a striped table. A line chart's area fill drops from 0.30 to 0.18, past which the eye starts reading the region's *area*, a quantity nobody measured.
    - **Legends and points read as a key, not as decoration.** A multi-series legend is centred over the plot rather than left-aligned under the title, where it read as a subtitle with dots in it. Line points are **rings** — card-coloured inside the series stroke — with the latest point the single filled disc on the chart; filled dots everywhere beaded the line and made "latest" a 1.5px radius difference nobody can see. A donut's legend is a two-column key carrying each slice's **share**, because a donut's subject is proportion and a legend of bare names hands the reader a colour key and asks them to eyeball angles. A percentage, never the raw value: the figures reach the renderer as numbers rather than the server-formatted strings a KPI carries, and a ratio needs no locale.

    **Amended 2026-09-03 (third) — "Meadow Lit": the wash comes back to the marks, and the matching problem is answered rather than avoided.** The palette is unchanged again — every hue, every slot, every semantic, and every measured margin above still stands; `scripts/validate_palette.js` is untouched and still passes, because nothing here introduces a hue. What is amended is the *flat marks* rule of the second amendment, on the finding that it over-corrected: a page of flat fills on flat rails reads as bland at exactly the data density this product runs at, which is the complaint it came back as.

    Marks are washed again, and the objection that removed the wash twice — that a reader matching a bar to its legend dot is matching a colour to the *midpoint of a wash* — is now fixed instead of dodged. Three properties make the wash safe where the five-stop cylinder and the two-stop sheen were not:

    - **Every stop is a lightness step of ONE audited hue** (`RAMP`), so hue and chroma are held across the whole mark and every CVD separation measured above is a separation between marks, not within one.
    - **The base sits at the 52% stop**, so the midpoint of the mark — the part the eye samples — is exactly the colour named in `SERIES`.
    - **The legend swatch is painted from the same stops** (`legendDotStyle`, sharing `markStops` with the SVG gradient so the two cannot be tuned apart). A reader matches a wash to a wash. This is the piece the two earlier attempts never had, and it is the whole of why this one binds.

    The lit end deliberately stops **six tenths of the way** to the ramp's `light` step: `light` is a cap sized for a donut wedge, which has area and a card-coloured hairline around it, and a 38px column painted in it dissolves into the card. The wash always runs **axis → tip**, so it reinforces the direction the length is read in. Rails, the magnitude ramp, the dashed grid, the centred legend and the ring/disc line points are all unchanged — this amendment touches paint, not structure.

    Two additions come with it:

    - **A trend line carries a soft drop shadow in its own hue** (`LineGlow`, `feDropShadow` at 0.28) and a stroke washed along the trend. A line is one 3px stroke on a white card, which is why a chart made only of lines reads thin however saturated it is. `print.css`'s `svg [filter]` guard strips this on paper, where a hue-coloured blur separates into a CMYK smudge — the guard was kept after the 2026-09-01 shadow was removed precisely so the next filter would be covered before it shipped, and it was. **Gradients are not stripped on paper**: a `linearGradient` is paint, not an effect, and removing it would make the export disagree with the page it was taken from (ADR-021).
    - **A KPI tile whose value is a bare percentage draws a dial** (`MeterRing`) — an arc of the metric's own hue on a rail of that hue, rounded cap, gradient sweep. The share is read back out of the **server's own formatted value** (`rateShare`, strict `^\d+(\.\d+)?%$`); there is no second numeric field on the widget and asking the server for one would be a second representation of a number it already sent, so the dial and the digits are the same value drawn twice and cannot disagree. Anything that is not a bare percentage gets no dial and the tile is laid out exactly as before. Attendance at 86% beside realisation at 53% is legible before either figure is read, which is the argument for it.

    **Amended 2026-09-03 (fourth) — the ACCENT set widens from four to nine. `SERIES` does not move.**

    Keep the two apart, because they answer different questions and only one of them is audited. `SERIES` orders five hues **semantically** (demand, collected, pending, defaulters, fifth) and its constraint is the separation between *adjacent marks inside one chart* — the hard one, since deuteranopia collapses green and ochre to a single hue and only their lightness gap survives. An **accent** colours a chart that has *one* series: there is nothing inside it to be confused with, and the only separation that matters is between one card and the card next to it. That is a far weaker requirement, and it is met by spreading hues around the wheel.

    The four accents were `primary` / `secondary` / `warning` / `negative`, and the last two were reserved for the cases where their meaning actually applied — so most preview cards fell back to `primary` and the Dashboard read as one blue page. A grid whose entire job is to hold eight different subjects side by side was painting six of them the same colour, which is the one thing that makes a set of cards unscannable. Five identity-only hues are added: **`violet` `#a886e5`** (the fifth series), **`indigo` `#5f8fe0`**, **`aqua` `#2fb3aa`**, **`coral` `#e8896b`**, **`plum` `#bd77d2`**. Each gets a `RAMP` entry built exactly as the series ramps are — lightness moved, hue and chroma held — so an accent and a series mark are painted by identical machinery. The lead stays teal and the new hues are led by the blues (`indigo` and `aqua` sit either side of teal), so the page still reads as one product with warm and purple counterweights rather than as a paint chart.

    **`warning` is the one accent that differs from its series slot,** and the reason is the CVD constraint itself. `SERIES[2]` is `#a17a00` and is that dark *on purpose*: it is the only thing separating "pending" from "collected" for a red-green colour-blind reader looking at a grouped bar. A single-series chart has no green beside it, so the lightness gap buys nothing there and the cost is real — Fee Defaulters' ageing chart rendered as four brown bars. The accent is therefore the lighter **`#d09a2c`**, applied only where the constraint does not hold; `SERIES[2]` is untouched, so every grouped bar in the product is exactly as it was. At 2.4:1 against the card it sits under the 3:1 line — the same accepted deviation `#72ba63` already carries, with the same relief (axis values, a title, a wash that deepens toward the axis). Its ramp's `deep` step is held high (`#b8862a`) because amber loses chroma into brown faster than any other hue here, and the magnitude ramp paints the tallest bar in `deep`.

    **Meaning still outranks arrangement, and that is what makes any of this safe.** `measureColour` lets a widget's own `tone` win over an accent, so a caller cycling hues across a grid can never repaint overdue money in the teal it used for headcounts. The same precedence now applies to a **KPI tile** (`kpiColour`): a strip may colour a tile by subject, but only where `tone` is `neutral` — which is not a judgement the server made, it is the absence of one (services/home.ts marks every Dashboard tile neutral precisely because no threshold makes a student count good or bad news). A tile the server marked `warning` or `negative` keeps the colour its meaning earned, whatever position it sits in. The Dashboard strip cycles `primary · indigo · aqua · violet · coral` **by position, not by metric**: a hue chosen per metric would be a second, client-side claim about what that metric means, and the browser is the one place this product refuses to put meaning.

    **Vertical category axes tilt only when they must (2026-09-03).** A label of six characters or fewer is drawn flat; longer ones keep the −35° tilt. Every such axis was tilted unconditionally, which is right for twelve month names in a half-width panel and pure chrome for four quarters, six academic years or twelve roman-numeral classes — it makes a reader tilt their head to read "Q1" and gives the panel a ragged bottom edge. Six characters is ~44px, which fits at every count and panel width the grid produces; the test is deliberately conservative, because this module does not know the panel's width (see `rotatedTicks`), so no axis that reads today gets worse.
3. **Tenant theming** — each school/trust gets accent color + logo (topbar, buttons, PDF header) from the same token system; no per-client CSS forks.
4. **Feels instant** — skeleton loaders, streamed AI widgets, optimistic filter changes; UI responds within 100 ms even while data loads.

They remain inside their modules too: a shortcut, not a second copy. Both rows are read out of the served catalog **by id** (`PINNED`, `Sidebar.tsx`), so a report renamed in the catalog renames itself here and one withheld drops out without leaving a row that goes nowhere. |

## 3. UX conventions (bind all screens)

- **Locked ≠ hidden, but a menu row is a promise (amended 2026-09-01):** a *gated* feature stays visible with a lock and a path to unlock — hiding it would hide the setting that opens it, and 🔒 → Settings drives setup better than hiding does. Ask AI is that case and, in the menu, now the only one. The rule does **not** extend to a feature nobody can open under any role, with any key, from any screen: those are withheld entirely. Concretely, `/api/home` serves only `status: 'available'` cards (`servedDashboards`, `apps/orchestrator/src/services/home.ts`), so both surfaces that list dashboards — the sidebar and Home's "More dashboards" strip — drop the other two states together. That covers `coming` (serving path unbuilt: Group Overview, Cross-School Attendance, School Comparison on the rollup store, ADR-010; Workflow Agents on the agent runtime, ADR-022) and `blocked` (the ERP extract holds no such data, AUDIT_REPORT C20: Exam Performance today), plus the sidebar's hand-written "Agents · SOON" row. The cards keep their entries, reasons and ADR references in the catalog, which is where the people who can act on them read them — and a card returns to the menu by turning `available`, with nothing to re-add by hand. **KPI tiles are unchanged:** `blocked_metrics` still renders "— · no data" on Home, because a tile explains a number the strip is visibly missing, where a menu row promises a screen that does not exist. **Module tiles take the same carve-out (added 2026-09-01).** `servedModules` serves a module whose every report is withheld as `status: 'empty'`, with the withheld reports' own reasons on it, and the tile does not click. The seven modules are a fixed description of a school's world rather than a list of destinations — drop Exam and six tiles assert that schools have no exams; keep it and it states that the ERP sends no exam data, which is a fact only the extract's owner can change and only this tile can tell a school. The line that does not move: the tile quotes the CARD's reason and never writes one, so an arriving extract fills the tile in by flipping one `status`, exactly as it fills the menu.
- **A mark has to read as a mark (added 2026-09-01).** A bar is drawn thick enough to be a shape rather than a rule: **22px** for a single horizontal series, **16px** for one of several sharing a band, **38 / 26px** for the vertical equivalents (`BAR_PX`, `packages/chart-spec/src/react/widgets.tsx`), with a **4px rounded data-end and a square foot at the baseline** — rounded at both ends would float the bar off the axis it is measured from. These are caps, not widths: a chart with many categories still thins out on its own, and a band is budgeted at roughly bar-plus-half-a-bar so the leftover stays air. Past ~24px a bar stops being a mark and becomes a block, which is why the ceiling is where it is. The Dashboard preview grid was **2-up** (`.pgallery`) for the same reason: it went 4-5 → 3 → 2 columns, and each step was the same complaint — the chart inside the card was too small to read as a chart, and the preview IS the reason to click the card. **Amended 2026-09-03:** it is a 12-column bento (8 for a line, 6 for a bar, 4 for a ring — Dashboard/overview row above). The floor that argument established is intact and is what the spans respect: the narrowest slot the grid can produce is 4/12 of a ~1650px page, and it is only ever given to a ring, which is a fixed-size object with a key beside it and gains nothing from half a page. What the old rule missed is that not every chart wants the same width — a twelve-month trend is read left to right and gets better the longer it is, where a ring given 900px just sits in the middle of a lake of white. Below 1400px the bento collapses back to two equal halves, and below 980px to one card per row. A compact card's chart stretches to match a taller neighbour in its row but **no further than 1.3× its natural height**, so a 3-school chart sharing a row with an 8-department one keeps its rows together instead of drifting apart.
- **Scope is always on screen:** picker chip in the topbar; scope line under report titles; scope printed on PDFs.
- **Transparency affordances everywhere:** 🧠 View logic and ⧉ Clone appear on every report surface, including AI artifacts.
- **Status streaming for anything slow:** AI steps, agent test runs, PDF generation — never a bare spinner.
- **Destructive/irreversible actions** (publish agent, disable AI, disconnect channel) confirm consequences in plain language, and every such state has a reverse path.
- **Empty states teach:** My Reports and Agents explain how their first item is created.
- **Two-up is the default rhythm; full width is earned from the data (added 2026-09-03, second UI pass).** Amends the footprint rule below. Every panel variant is now **span 6** except `wide`, which stays 12. `hero` (7) and `side` (5) existed to pair one line chart with one donut, and that single pairing bought an off-grid rhythm the rest of the page had to work around — a 7 followed by anything but a 5 stranded columns, and `dense` papered over it by pulling later panels out of the server's order. Equal halves pair with any neighbour. **What earns a full row is the widget's DATA, not its kind**, and which part of the data depends on which way its categories run (`needsFullWidth`, `packages/chart-spec/src/react/widgets.tsx`): on a **horizontal** bar the categories run down the panel, so their number sets the height and only label length competes for width (`longest > 16`) — fifteen classes labelled "NURSERY" read fine in half a page where five departments labelled "ADHOC OFFICE STAFF(A.O.S)" do not; on a **vertical** axis, and on a line's periods, every category needs its own slice of width, so count counts too (`longest > 16 || count > 14`) — 78 months in half a page is a smear. A **table** earns 12 by carrying more than four columns: eleven columns of figures cannot be read in half a page, three columns of short strings were taking a full row for nothing. **A drill panel is now 6 at every level, where it was 12.** The stability requirement of the rule below is unchanged and is still the point — a panel that resizes as the reader descends is not "in place" — but 12 was only ever the span both orientations could share back when horizontal meant 12 and vertical meant 6. With both now 6 unless their data earns more, 6 is equally stable and costs half the screen; and the span is fixed for the whole descent rather than re-derived per level, which would reintroduce exactly the resizing being avoided. The driver is screen space: on a 1900px page a full-width panel holding three bars spends about a thousand horizontal pixels on nothing, and a reader comparing two panels scrolls between them instead of putting them side by side, which is most of what a reporting surface is for.
- **Density is the house style, and the first UI pass overshot it (added 2026-09-03).** That pass made the charts quieter and then spent the quiet on air — radius 12→14, panel padding 20/22, grid gap 16→20, KPI figure 26→29, table rows 9→12 — on the theory that a quiet card takes its definition from whitespace. On a page of eleven panels what it actually bought was a longer page with less on each screen, which is the generic SaaS admin look rather than an analytics product. Corrected: **card radius 8** (what §1 said all along; the 14 was drift), **shadows reduced to a hairline** because a panel is delimited by its 1px border and the canvas behind it and a shadow on top of a border says the same thing twice, **grid gap 12**, **panel padding 12/14**, **panel title 13px**, **KPI figure 24px on 11/12px padding**, **table rows 7px at 12.5px**. Card entrances fade rather than rise, and a KPI tile no longer lifts on hover: a tile is not a control, so movement under the pointer promises an interaction that does not exist. A preview card still responds, because clicking it does open the report — with a border, not a 3px rise. The rule these follow: **frame is not content.** Every pixel of padding, radius, shadow and gap is a pixel the charts, KPIs and tables do not get, and they are the reason the page exists.
- **A panel's footprint comes from the widget, never from the report (added 2026-08-31).** Report bodies are a 12-column dense grid (`.specPanels`). The renderer, not the spec, decides each panel's span — a vertical bar is 6, a table or a horizontal bar is 12, and a line is 7 so that a donut's 5 completes its row (Fee Collection's "Receipts by month" beside "Payment modes" is the reference pair). A spec that could pin its own width would let a saved report outlive a layout change, exactly as a spec that could pin a colour would outlive a palette audit — so it cannot. The rule follows from the widget's own SHAPE, the same way a bar's orientation follows from its label lengths: a **multi-series** line is 12, because it carries a legend and twice the marks, the reader is matching two shapes point by point, and a report comparing two years has no five-slice breakdown to pair with — at 7 it strands 5 columns that no later panel can fill, since every remaining panel is 12. A **drill panel** (any level of an ADR-020 path) is 12 at every level regardless of orientation, because a path changes its categories as it descends — Comparative Analysis runs "World School" → "April 2026-27" → "NURSERY", so orientation alone would size it 12, 12, then 6, halving the panel under the reader on the last click and freeing six columns nothing below can fill. docs/06 §4.4 has a drill *swap the chart in place*, and a panel that resizes as you descend is not in place: the page reflows under the cursor that just clicked, and ← Back reflows it again. A stable frame is what makes three levels read as one chart being narrowed rather than three charts. **The KPI strip is the exception, amended 2026-09-01: every tile is the same size, at every count.** The first tile used to render double-width at a 36px figure, capped at five tiles because a 2× lead broke a six-tile strip into 5 + 1 — and that cap was the rule arguing against itself, since emphasis you have to switch off as the strip grows is a layout constraint dressed as a reading order. On Dashboard's four cards it read as an accident before it read as emphasis: one wide tile beside three narrow ones sends the eye to the odd shape rather than the number in it. A strip ranks by **order** (the server's `spec.widgets`, contract) and signals by **tone** (the left-edge colour, from meaning the server assigned); neither needs a tile to change size, and both survive the strip growing by a card. `.kpi` is now one rule — `flex: 1 1 190px` on every tile, no per-tile override — so four tiles divide the row in four and six divide it in six. Paper agrees (print.css's fixed 4-column grid); the unavailable-metric tile agrees too, its figure raised 24px → 26px to match a live one, since what marks it unavailable is its muted colour and `NO DATA` pill.

## 4. Assumptions

Desktop-first (admin work); layouts degrade to single-column at ~980 px; the Ask-AI split becomes tabs on mobile. Localization (Hindi/regional) is anticipated in message templates (AI-compose language option) before full UI localization.
