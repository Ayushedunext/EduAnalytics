# 10 — UI/UX & Design System

## 1. Design language

Clean, whitespace-heavy, card-based; the AI is the hero feature but nothing depends on it. Reference feel: *Linear's calm + Stripe Dashboard's data density, tuned for school administrators — friendly, not fintech-cold.*

### Tokens (source of truth)

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Teal | `#008a9d` | Primary actions, primary chart series (was `#028090` until 2026-09-01 — it measured OKLCH chroma 0.095, under the 0.10 floor at which a colour starts reading grey) |
| Ink | `#032E36` | Headings, sidebar/nav, dark bands |
| Seafoam | `#048E53` | Secondary accents (emerald's deep ramp step) |
| Mint | `#3DB575` | Success, active/ON states, "collected" |
| Amber | `#C78100` | Warnings, fees-outstanding, third chart series (was `#F2A93B` — lightness 0.787, outside the 0.43–0.77 band) |
| Red | `#C74859` | Errors, defaulter counts, FALSE branches |
| Indigo | `#4967C6` | The fifth chart series. No fixed meaning — added 2026-09-01 |
| Slate `#334155` / Muted `#64748B` | Body text / captions |
| Canvas `#F1F5F9` / Card `#FFFFFF` | Page background / cards |

Type: **Inter**, one scale — Page title 28 semibold · Card heading 18 semibold · Body/table 14 · Caption/kicker 11 (+2 tracking); tabular figures for all numbers. Components: 8 px radius cards with soft shadows; primary (filled teal) / secondary (teal outline) / ghost buttons; chips; KPI cards (big number + delta pill); switches.

**Surfaces amended 2026-09-03**, alongside the chart-paint amendment in principle 2 below and for the same reason — the charts got quieter, and chrome that was merely *present* before became the loudest thing on the page once it no longer had gloss to compete with. Card radius **14 px** (from 12: at the 340–720 px a panel occupies, 12 reads as a square with its corners knocked off, and chips keep the 7 px `--radius-sm`, so "small control" and "surface" are now visibly different rather than two pixels apart). Card shadows roughly **halved** — a dashboard is cards on a tinted canvas and the canvas already separates them; at the old weights eleven panels read as a stack of floating slabs. `--shadow-float` is untouched: a popover really is floating. Panel padding 20/22 and grid gap **20 px** (from 16), because a quiet card takes its definition from its whitespace rather than its edges. A KPI figure is **29 px** (from 26) over an 11 px tracked kicker on a 3 px rhythm — the figure is the reason the tile exists and was sitting at barely twice its own label. A table's header is a **label strip, not a filled band**: sticky and card-coloured with a single rule under smaller, tracked, muted type, where it used to carry a grey fill that was the loudest horizontal element on a card whose subject is the rows below it; rows go to 12 px of vertical padding with a lighter divider, since a column of figures is scanned down and what makes that easy is air between lines, not rules.

### Principles

1. **Calm surfaces** — data is the color, chrome is quiet.
2. **One chart language** — every report, custom report, AI artifact and PDF draws through one renderer, so they cannot drift (a direct consequence of spec-driven rendering, doc 05). **Amended 2026-09-01.** Categorical series are **five** steps (`#008a9d`, `#3db575`, `#c78100`, `#c74859`, `#4967c6`), each with a three-step ramp (deep / base / lit) used to shade marks. The prior four-step set was capped at four because a 2026-08-22 audit found two teal-family steps indistinguishable at mark size, and §1.2's "teal-family series" rule ruled out reaching outside the family to fix the spacing. That rule is what is amended: **hue variety is now allowed beyond the teal family**, because leaving the family is exactly what buys the separation the old audit could not find inside it — indigo is nowhere near teal, where a second teal step never could be. The slot ORDER remains semantic and is not permutable (demand, collected, pending, defaulters), so hues are pushed apart within their families and lightness carries the separation that hue cannot: under protanopia and deuteranopia green and amber collapse to one hue, and only their lightness gap survives. Measured on the shipped set (`scripts/validate_palette.js`): worst adjacent pair ΔE 9.1 deutan / 9.8 tritan, worst normal-vision pair ΔE 16.2, against floors of 8 and 15; all five inside the lightness band and above the chroma floor, which the old set failed on two slots and one slot respectively. One accepted deviation, stated rather than hidden: `#3db575` sits at 2.54:1 against the card, under the 3:1 relief line — the remedy is visible labels or a table view, and every multi-series chart here carries a mandatory legend, axis values and a tooltip. The old palette failed that check on **two** slots. A category past the fifth is still never a generated hue; it folds into a recessive "Other" fill (`#64748b` at reduced opacity), alternating with `#cbd5e1` inside a stack.

    **Amended 2026-09-03 — how a mark is PAINTED, which is a separate question from which hue it takes.** The palette above is unchanged; every hue, its slot and its semantics stand. What changed is the treatment, after a review against a reference dashboard whose charts read markedly cleaner than ours at the same data density. Four rules now bind, all in `packages/chart-spec/src/react/widgets.tsx`:

    - **A mark is its hue, not a rendering of a solid.** A bar's fill is a **two-stop** gradient across its thickness — the ramp's new half-step `soft` at the lit edge, `base` at the far edge — and a donut slice takes the same two stops. It was a five-stop cylinder (deep → base → lit → base → deep) plus a hue-coloured drop shadow, and that read exactly as designed: as tubes. The whole of a bar's meaning is where its end lands, and shading that models a *surface* spends the reader's attention on the middle of the mark; three tubes side by side in a grouped chart also fought each other, and fourteen of them read as plumbing. The two-stop version keeps the one thing the shading bought — a lit edge, so a mark sits on the card rather than being a hole cut in it — and drops the rest. It also fixes a real defect: a five-stop wash ending on `deep` meant a reader matching a bar against its legend dot was matching two different colours. The `deep` and `lit` steps remain, for pressed states, KPI tile edges and the donut. The ramp gains **`soft`** (`#2f9eb0`, `#5fc98e`, `#d99833`, `#d96471`, `#607ed6`) because painting a 22px bar from `lit` made it visibly two-tone.
    - **A single-series bar chart shades its bars along the series' own ramp by magnitude**, `soft` at the bottom of the range to `deep` at the top, measured from zero exactly as the bar length is. This is *not* the "paint the tallest bar darker" device removed on 2026-09-01: that singled out one mark on a single-measure chart and therefore read as a second series. A continuous ramp picks out nothing, groups nothing, and is a function of the same number the length already encodes — redundant with it, which is what makes it safe. It exists because fourteen classes in one flat teal is a picket fence with no focus, and single-series bar charts are what this product has most of.
    - **Chrome is subtracted from every chart.** Grid lines are dashed (`3 4`) and lightened to `#e3e8ef` — a grid is a ruler, and a solid rule inside a card that already has a border reads as a second box. Axis lines and tick stubs are gone entirely: the card is the frame and the grid is the scale, so a third boundary says nothing new. The per-bar background "track" is gone — on three schools it framed the marks, on fourteen classes it drew fourteen full-width grey rails behind the data and the panel read as a striped table. A line chart's area fill drops from 0.30 to 0.18, past which the eye starts reading the region's *area*, a quantity nobody measured.
    - **Legends and points read as a key, not as decoration.** A multi-series legend is centred over the plot rather than left-aligned under the title, where it read as a subtitle with dots in it. Line points are **rings** — card-coloured inside the series stroke — with the latest point the single filled disc on the chart; filled dots everywhere beaded the line and made "latest" a 1.5px radius difference nobody can see. A donut's legend is a two-column key carrying each slice's **share**, because a donut's subject is proportion and a legend of bare names hands the reader a colour key and asks them to eyeball angles. A percentage, never the raw value: the figures reach the renderer as numbers rather than the server-formatted strings a KPI carries, and a ratio needs no locale.

    **Vertical category axes tilt only when they must (2026-09-03).** A label of six characters or fewer is drawn flat; longer ones keep the −35° tilt. Every such axis was tilted unconditionally, which is right for twelve month names in a half-width panel and pure chrome for four quarters, six academic years or twelve roman-numeral classes — it makes a reader tilt their head to read "Q1" and gives the panel a ragged bottom edge. Six characters is ~44px, which fits at every count and panel width the grid produces; the test is deliberately conservative, because this module does not know the panel's width (see `rotatedTicks`), so no axis that reads today gets worse.
3. **Tenant theming** — each school/trust gets accent color + logo (topbar, buttons, PDF header) from the same token system; no per-client CSS forks.
4. **Feels instant** — skeleton loaders, streamed AI widgets, optimistic filter changes; UI responds within 100 ms even while data loads.

## 3. UX conventions (bind all screens)

- **Locked ≠ hidden, but a menu row is a promise (amended 2026-09-01):** a *gated* feature stays visible with a lock and a path to unlock — hiding it would hide the setting that opens it, and 🔒 → Settings drives setup better than hiding does. Ask AI is that case and, in the menu, now the only one. The rule does **not** extend to a feature nobody can open under any role, with any key, from any screen: those are withheld entirely. Concretely, `/api/home` serves only `status: 'available'` cards (`servedDashboards`, `apps/orchestrator/src/services/home.ts`), so both surfaces that list dashboards — the sidebar and Home's "More dashboards" strip — drop the other two states together. That covers `coming` (serving path unbuilt: Group Overview, Cross-School Attendance, School Comparison on the rollup store, ADR-010; Workflow Agents on the agent runtime, ADR-022) and `blocked` (the ERP extract holds no such data, AUDIT_REPORT C20: Exam Performance today), plus the sidebar's hand-written "Agents · SOON" row. The cards keep their entries, reasons and ADR references in the catalog, which is where the people who can act on them read them — and a card returns to the menu by turning `available`, with nothing to re-add by hand. **KPI tiles are unchanged:** `blocked_metrics` still renders "— · no data" on Home, because a tile explains a number the strip is visibly missing, where a menu row promises a screen that does not exist. **Module tiles take the same carve-out (added 2026-09-01).** `servedModules` serves a module whose every report is withheld as `status: 'empty'`, with the withheld reports' own reasons on it, and the tile does not click. The seven modules are a fixed description of a school's world rather than a list of destinations — drop Exam and six tiles assert that schools have no exams; keep it and it states that the ERP sends no exam data, which is a fact only the extract's owner can change and only this tile can tell a school. The line that does not move: the tile quotes the CARD's reason and never writes one, so an arriving extract fills the tile in by flipping one `status`, exactly as it fills the menu.
- **A mark has to read as a mark (added 2026-09-01).** A bar is drawn thick enough to be a shape rather than a rule: **22px** for a single horizontal series, **16px** for one of several sharing a band, **38 / 26px** for the vertical equivalents (`BAR_PX`, `packages/chart-spec/src/react/widgets.tsx`), with a **4px rounded data-end and a square foot at the baseline** — rounded at both ends would float the bar off the axis it is measured from. These are caps, not widths: a chart with many categories still thins out on its own, and a band is budgeted at roughly bar-plus-half-a-bar so the leftover stays air. Past ~24px a bar stops being a mark and becomes a block, which is why the ceiling is where it is. The Dashboard preview grid is **2-up** (`.pgallery`) for the same reason: it went 4-5 → 3 → 2 columns, and each step was the same complaint — the chart inside the card was too small to read as a chart, and the preview IS the reason to click the card. A compact card's chart stretches to match a taller neighbour in its row but **no further than 1.3× its natural height**, so a 3-school chart sharing a row with an 8-department one keeps its rows together instead of drifting apart.
- **Scope is always on screen:** picker chip in the topbar; scope line under report titles; scope printed on PDFs.
- **Transparency affordances everywhere:** 🧠 View logic and ⧉ Clone appear on every report surface, including AI artifacts.
- **Status streaming for anything slow:** AI steps, agent test runs, PDF generation — never a bare spinner.
- **Destructive/irreversible actions** (publish agent, disable AI, disconnect channel) confirm consequences in plain language, and every such state has a reverse path.
- **Empty states teach:** My Reports and Agents explain how their first item is created.
- **A panel's footprint comes from the widget, never from the report (added 2026-08-31).** Report bodies are a 12-column dense grid (`.specPanels`). The renderer, not the spec, decides each panel's span — a vertical bar is 6, a table or a horizontal bar is 12, and a line is 7 so that a donut's 5 completes its row (Fee Collection's "Receipts by month" beside "Payment modes" is the reference pair). A spec that could pin its own width would let a saved report outlive a layout change, exactly as a spec that could pin a colour would outlive a palette audit — so it cannot. The rule follows from the widget's own SHAPE, the same way a bar's orientation follows from its label lengths: a **multi-series** line is 12, because it carries a legend and twice the marks, the reader is matching two shapes point by point, and a report comparing two years has no five-slice breakdown to pair with — at 7 it strands 5 columns that no later panel can fill, since every remaining panel is 12. A **drill panel** (any level of an ADR-020 path) is 12 at every level regardless of orientation, because a path changes its categories as it descends — Comparative Analysis runs "World School" → "April 2026-27" → "NURSERY", so orientation alone would size it 12, 12, then 6, halving the panel under the reader on the last click and freeing six columns nothing below can fill. docs/06 §4.4 has a drill *swap the chart in place*, and a panel that resizes as you descend is not in place: the page reflows under the cursor that just clicked, and ← Back reflows it again. A stable frame is what makes three levels read as one chart being narrowed rather than three charts. **The KPI strip is the exception, amended 2026-09-01: every tile is the same size, at every count.** The first tile used to render double-width at a 36px figure, capped at five tiles because a 2× lead broke a six-tile strip into 5 + 1 — and that cap was the rule arguing against itself, since emphasis you have to switch off as the strip grows is a layout constraint dressed as a reading order. On Dashboard's four cards it read as an accident before it read as emphasis: one wide tile beside three narrow ones sends the eye to the odd shape rather than the number in it. A strip ranks by **order** (the server's `spec.widgets`, contract) and signals by **tone** (the left-edge colour, from meaning the server assigned); neither needs a tile to change size, and both survive the strip growing by a card. `.kpi` is now one rule — `flex: 1 1 190px` on every tile, no per-tile override — so four tiles divide the row in four and six divide it in six. Paper agrees (print.css's fixed 4-column grid); the unavailable-metric tile agrees too, its figure raised 24px → 26px to match a live one, since what marks it unavailable is its muted colour and `NO DATA` pill.

## 4. Assumptions

Desktop-first (admin work); layouts degrade to single-column at ~980 px; the Ask-AI split becomes tabs on mobile. Localization (Hindi/regional) is anticipated in message templates (AI-compose language option) before full UI localization.
